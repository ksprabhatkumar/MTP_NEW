Wearable Human Activity Recognition Utilizing Residual Dynamic Graphs and 1D Causal State-Space Models
The transition from visual skeleton-based action recognition to Wearable Human Activity Recognition (WHAR) utilizing one-dimensional Inertial Measurement Unit (IMU) time-series data presents substantial biomechanical and computational challenges. Traditional spatial-temporal graph convolutional networks (ST-GCNs) rely on rigid, predefined topologies derived from physical human anatomy. However, continuous 1D IMU data streams, particularly those corrupted by asynchronous sampling or severe sensor displacement, fail to map cleanly to fixed adjacency matrices.
To address the sensor displacement and placement agnosticism problems, current research indicates a paradigm shift toward dynamic, learnable topological connections. Formulating a dense adjacency matrix as a residual combination—specifically   —allows deep learning architectures to dynamically route information across varied sensor locations. Furthermore, processing these extracted dynamic spatial graphs through a 1D Causal Mamba (State-Space Model) block theoretically circumvents the quadratic temporal complexity of Transformer architectures, enabling linear-time sequence modeling for high-frequency continuous data.
The following exhaustive literature review synthesizes recent advancements across IEEE, ACM, arXiv, and MDPI (2019–2026), systematically categorizing the research into Dynamic Graph Kinematics, Sensor Displacement Mitigation, Visual-to-1D Translation, and the intersection of Graph Neural Networks with State-Space Models.

Early skeletal models relied on unweighted, static Laplacian matrices. However, human motion is intrinsically correlated across non-adjacent nodes (e.g., the synchronized movement of the left wrist and right ankle during walking). 	2. Models Tackling Sensor Displacement and Placement Agnosticism
In wearable computing, sensor displacement—where IMUs shift on the user's body, rotate out of axial alignment, or suffer packet loss—causes severe spatial distribution shifts. Traditional machine learning models trained on highly calibrated, strictly placed sensors suffer catastrophic performance drops when deployed in real-world scenarios. Addressing placement agnosticism requires networks capable of dynamically rerouting physical topologies on the fly, rendering the model invariant to localized coordinate frame mismatches.
[Dealing with the Effects of Sensor Displacement in Wearable Activity Recognition]
               * Published Year: 2024
               * Methodology Used: This framework treats multi-node wearable sensing systems as physically interconnected network topologies to combat missing or displaced modalities. It introduces a graph recovery module based on GNNs that relies on a spatial locality assumption to explicitly map data correlations between interconnected sensors13. The methodology utilizes a neural message-passing mechanism mirroring the physical network. Crucially, a Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU)-inspired gating mechanism automatically controls the information flow between available, stable sensors and displaced/missing sensors, mathematically reconstructing corrupted feature spaces prior to classification13.

               * How this can be applied to our project: The GRU-based missing-sensor gating mechanism can be injected into the    matrix formulation via a binary missing-modality mask, ensuring that gradients for non-responsive IMU nodes are routed to adjacent stable nodes.

Subsequently, the MHA mechanism computes attention weights across the spatial graph node embeddings to learn cross-sensor dependencies. 3. Translation of Visual Skeleton Adaptive Graphs to Continuous 1D IMU Sensor Data
The vast majority of dynamic graph kinematics research is built upon 3D visual skeleton data (e.g.,    spatial joint coordinates extracted from RGB-D cameras). Translating these theories to 1D IMU data requires converting continuous, high-frequency signals (accelerometer, gyroscope, and magnetometer readings) into structural nodes. IMU data is not natively graph-structured; it consists of multivariate time-series arrays. Establishing an isomorphic relationship between camera-based joints and IMU-based time-series channels is paramount.
[Activity Graph based Convolutional Neural Network for Human Activity Recognition using Acceleration and Gyroscope Data]
                        * Published Year: 2022
                        * Methodology Used: This paper generates highly optimized "activity graphs" directly from 1D multi-axis acceleration and gyroscope data. The underlying assumption is that latent physical correlations exist between human movement and the phase alignments of multiple sensing signals19. The methodology employs a specialized three-step sorting algorithm to convert unordered, multi-axis 1D time-series data into a structured 2D spatial graph. By sorting and stacking the signals along their width and height dynamically based on signal extension characteristics, the network produces a mathematically optimized baseline activity graph, which is then traversed by a deep CNN to extract discriminative features5.
                        * Dataset Used: UCI HAR, WISDM, Local Smartphone Datasets
                        * How this can be applied to our project: The three-step sorting algorithm for stacking multi-axis signals provides a definitive mathematical blueprint for constructing the    matrix, ensuring that the initial topology correctly maps the physical proximity of IMU X, Y, and Z axes.

	4. Intersection of Dynamic Graphs and State-Space Models (Mamba) for Time-Series

State-Space Models (SSMs)—specifically the Mamba architecture—utilize an input-dependent selective scanning mechanism that allows for linear-time    sequential processing.
