ABSTRACT
Wearable Human Activity Recognition (WHAR) utilizing Inertial Measurement
Units (IMUs) is a foundational capability for ambient healthcare, industrial er-
gonomics, and ubiquitous edge computing. However, current state-of-the-art deep
learning architectures suffer from two debilitating limitations: extreme inference
latency driven by sequential Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs/LSTMs), and
catastrophic fragility when confronted with real-world hardware failures, such as
sensor battery depletion or network dropouts.
This research proposes GAE-Mamba-WHAR, a novel, end-to-end deep learning
framework designed to establish a new Pareto-optimal frontier for ubiquitous com-
puting. To resolve the sequential bottleneck of legacy models, the architecture
replaces traditional recurrent memory blocks with a 1D Causal Selective State-
Space Model (Mamba). By leveraging hardware-aware parallel associative scans
alongside Global Average Pooling (GAP) and Focal Loss, the framework achieves
a 40.6x reduction in inference latency (from 465.63 ms down to 11.45 ms) while
maintaining strict real-time causality.
To address physical hardware fragility, the framework introduces a Generative
Graph Autoencoder (GAE) utilizing Graph Isomorphism Networks (GINs) trained
via a joint cross-entropy and Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss function. The GAE
acts as a ”self-healing” imputation engine, intelligently predicting and hallucinat-
ing missing sensor data by mathematically modeling the structural kinematics of
surviving anatomical nodes.
9

PAGE 10
Evaluated under a strict Leave-One-Subject-Out (LOSO) cross-validation protocol
across three diverse benchmark datasets (DSADS, OPPORTUNITY, and REALD-
ISP), the proposed architecture demonstrates unprecedented cross-subject general-
ization, achieving up to 90.56% Macro-F1 on DSADS and 98.11% on REALDISP.
Crucially, during a simulated hardware degradation benchmark, the generative
architecture successfully retained an 89.12% Macro-F1 score even when suffering
a catastrophic failure of multiple physical sensors, proving its supreme robustness
and viability for continuous, real-world edge deployment.

Introduction
1.1 The Ubiquity of Wearable Human Activity
Recognition (WHAR)
The rapid proliferation of ubiquitous computing, mobile edge intelligence, and
Internet-of-Things (IoT) ecosystems has established Wearable Human Activity
Recognition (WHAR) as a cornerstone of modern ambient intelligence. By uti-
lizing high-frequency Inertial Measurement Units (IMUs)—primarily tri-axial ac-
celerometers, gyroscopes, and magnetometers—researchers can extract highly ac-
curate, semantically meaningful interpretations of human kinematics. The ap-
plication vectors for WHAR are vast and critical, spanning continuous mobile
healthcare monitoring, neurodegenerative disease tracking, industrial ergonomics,
ambient assisted living, and high-performance sports analytics.
17

PAGE 18
Historically, WHAR pipelines relied extensively on handcrafted statistical and
frequency-domain heuristics parsed through shallow machine learning algorithms
(e.g., Random Forests or Support Vector Machines). However, the paradigm has
decisively shifted toward end-to-end deep representational learning. Modern ar-
chitectures treat the human body as a complex biomechanical system, utilizing
deep neural networks to automatically map raw, multivariate sensor streams into
distinct activity classifications. Despite these advancements, deploying these deep
learning models into real-world, uncontrolled environments introduces severe com-
putational and physical challenges that remain largely unresolved in contemporary
literature.
1.2 Architectural Bottlenecks: The Sequence Mod-
eling Problem
The foundational era of deep learning in WHAR was dominated by Convolutional
Neural Networks (CNNs) for spatial feature extraction, coupled with Recurrent
Neural Networks (RNNs)—specifically Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) and
Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) networks—for sequential temporal modeling. While
CNN-RNN hybrid architectures like DeepConvLSTM and modern Graph-RNN
hybrids like GT-WHAR achieve excellent predictive accuracy, they suffer from a
fundamental architectural flaw: the recurrent sequential bottleneck.
Standard RNNs process time-series data sequentially; the hidden state at
timesteptcannot be computed until the operation at timestept−1 has con-
cluded. In the context of WHAR, where a typical sliding window may contain
125 to 250 timesteps, this sequential unrolling completely prevents parallel com-
putation. Consequently, recurrent models impose massive inference latency and
computational overhead. Conversely, while self-attention mechanisms and Trans-
former architectures resolve this sequential bottleneck by processing entire se-
quences simultaneously, their quadratic computational and memory complexity
18

PAGE 19
with respect to sequence length renders them incompatible with the strict Static
Random-Access Memory (SRAM) and micro-watt power budgets of edge-deployed
wearable microcontrollers. Thus, a critical need exists for a temporal architecture
that provides parallel sequence scanning without the quadratic memory constraints
of Transformers.
1.3 Hardware Vulnerabilities: Sensor Displace-
ment and Failure
Beyond algorithmic efficiency, WHAR systems face profound physical vulnerabil-
ities when deployed ”in the wild. ” In controlled laboratory settings, subjects wear
perfectly calibrated sensor arrays tightly strapped to specific anatomical nodes.
In real-world deployments, users are prone to imperfect sensor placement, varying
carrying positions, and outright hardware failures.
Most contemporary multi-sensor architectures assume a pristine, uninterrupted
data matrix. If a single sensor node—such as a smartwatch or a Bluetooth-
connected ankle IMU—experiences battery depletion or packet loss, the network
receives a stream of zeros or NaN (Not-a-Number) values. In standard Convo-
lutional or Graph Neural Networks, this corrupted node mathematically poisons
the feature maps of adjacent nodes, leading to catastrophic accuracy collapse.
Resolving this fragility requires moving beyond discriminative classification and
introducing Generative Artificial Intelligence capable of spatial imputation and
”self-healing” prior to temporal processing.
Motivation and Problem Statement This project is motivated by the ”Inference-
Robustness Tradeoff” currently plaguing wearable AI. Legacy models are compu-
tationally lightweight but lack the spatial topological awareness required for high
accuracy on complex, imbalanced datasets. Conversely, modern Graph-RNN mod-
els achieve high accuracy but suffer from severe sequential latency (often exceeding
450 ms per batch) and complete fragility in the face of hardware sensor drops.

PAGE 20
Therefore, the problem statement of this colloquium research is twofold:
1. How can we eliminate the recurrent temporal bottleneck to achieve ultra-low
latency, real-time edge inference without sacrificing the spatial accuracy of
structural Graph Neural Networks?
2. How can we mathematically immunize the architecture against partial hard-
ware failure and disconnected sensors in highly unpredictable real-world en-
vironments?
1.4 Project Contributions
To resolve these core challenges, this research proposes a novel, end-to-end deep
learning framework:GAE-Mamba-WHAR. This framework successfully unifies
Generative Graph Autoencoders with Selective State-Space Models to establish a
new Pareto-optimal frontier for ubiquitous computing.

Literature Review
Wearable Human Activity Recognition (WHAR) continuously evolves to balance
spatial representational capacity, temporal sequence modeling, and computational
efficiency for edge deployment. This chapter critically examines the transition from
heuristic algorithms to deep learning, the pivot toward Graph Neural Networks
(GNNs), and the recent advent of State-Space Models (SSMs). Through this eval-
uation, a comprehensive narrative is constructed to identify unresolved research
gaps hindering real-time, robust ubiquitous computing.
2.1 Evolution of Deep Learning in WHAR
Historically, WHAR relied on the manual extraction of statistical and frequency-
domain metrics from fixed-length sliding windows. While effective for classify-
ing structured, repetitive activities, these pipelines proved exceptionally brittle
against complex, overlapping human movements. The integration of deep learn-
23

PAGE 24
ing catalyzed a paradigm shift, primarily driven by Convolutional Neural Networks
(CNNs) automating hierarchical feature extraction. However, restricted receptive
fields limited their capacity to learn long-range temporal dependencies, precipi-
tating the rise of hybrid CNN-RNN architectures.
The seminal DeepConvLSTM framework [1] established the modern WHAR
blueprint by stacking convolutional layers for spatial extraction and Long Short-
Term Memory (LSTM) layers for chronological dependencies. Subsequent mod-
els, such as CAE-LSTM (2022), added encoder-decoder topologies to improve
noise resilience. Despite their historical dominance, these legacy architectures
are fundamentally constrained by two debilitating flaws. First, they suffer from
severe spatial blindness, treating multi-sensor inputs as flat grids and ignoring
the explicit kinematic linkages of human anatomy. Second, they face an insur-
mountable inference latency bottleneck driven by the mathematically sequential,
non-parallelizable nature of RNNs/LSTMs, draining battery capacity on resource-
constrained TinyML edge devices.
2.2 Spatial Kinematics via Graph Neural Net-
works (GNNs)
To address this inherent structural blindness, the WHAR domain transitioned
toward modeling the human body as a non-Euclidean graph, deploying Graph
Neural Networks (GNNs) to explicitly encode kinematic dependencies. Graph-
ConvLSTM [2] pioneered this by applying Spatio-Temporal GCNs across a bio-
logical adjacency matrix to update node representations via physical neighbors.
More recently, GT-WHAR [3] introduced dynamic frameworks utilizing adaptive
Graph Isomorphism Networks (GINs) to fluidly adjust spatial routing based on
the user’s instantaneous kinematic state.
Despite achieving true spatial awareness, these frameworks remain deeply crip-
pled by their legacy approach to temporal modeling. Both GraphConvLSTM
24

PAGE 25
and GT-WHAR rely predominantly on bidirectional LSTMs or Gated Recurrent
Units (GRUs) to process the temporal evolution of graph embeddings. The in-
tegration of computationally heavy graph convolutions with sequential recurrent
layers severely compounds the latency issue, rendering GNN-based architectures
unsuited for extreme network edge deployment.
2.3 The State-Space Model Paradigm Shift
In response to recurrent computational barriers, the deep learning community
introduced Structured State-Space Models (SSMs). The Mamba architecture fun-
damentally transformed sequence modeling by processing continuous-time differ-
ential systems via hardware-aware parallel prefix scans, achieving infinite context
windows with linear-timeO(N) complexity [4].
Recent WHAR architectures leverage this efficiency. BabyMamba-HAR [5] in-
troduced compressed configurations for microcontrollers using Channel-Independent
convolutional stems. DecomposeWHAR [6] and Dynamic-Attentive Selective Mamba
(DASM) [7] optimized feature extraction via depth-separable and group-aware
convolutions prior to state-space scanning. However, these models exhibit two
glaring limitations. First, in their pursuit of linear efficiency, they abandon anatom-
ical graph topologies, treating sensors as disassociated streams. Second, they over-
whelmingly rely on ”Bidirectional” Mamba scans. While mathematically sound
offline, backward scanning violates the strict causality (the Arrow of Time) re-
quired for real-time live event streaming.
2.4 Dealing with Missing Data in Ubiquitous Com-
puting
In unconstrained real-world environments, distributed body-worn sensors suffer
unpredictable hardware degradation, including battery depletion and Bluetooth
25

PAGE 26
packet loss. Legacy WHAR models, relying on naive linear interpolation, generally
crash or fail catastrophically when entire sensor nodes drop.
In broader spatiotemporal domains, Generative Graph Autoencoders success-
fully combat this. The PoGeVon framework [8], for example, utilizes Position-
aware Graph Enhanced Variational Autoencoders to accurately ”hallucinate” miss-
ing networked time-series data based on the highly correlated behavior of the sur-
viving topology. Currently, a profound gap exists at the intersection of generative
graphs, SSMs, and human kinematics: WHAR models lack a generative frame-
work capable of recognizing a hardware failure, instantly routing around the dead
node using graph priors, and seamlessly hallucinating the missing kinematic data
via an ultra-fast causal SSM.
2.5 Synthesis and Identification of Research Gaps
The critical evaluation of WHAR architectures identifies two massive, unresolved
research gaps that limit the field’s transition into robust ubiquitous computing:
1.The Pareto-Optimal Gap:Existing literature fails to reconcile spatial
precision with temporal efficiency. Researchers are forced to choose between
deep topological spatial awareness with severe recurrent latency (GNNs), or
ultra-low-latency temporal modeling that operates completely blind to kine-
matic linkages (SSMs). Furthermore, SOTA Mamba models rely on Bidi-
rectional scanning, violating real-time causality. A profound gap exists for
a Pareto-optimal architecture mathematically embedding anatomical graph
routing directly into a purely Causal Selective State-Space Model.
2.The Hardware Robustness Gap:Current WHAR models implicitly as-
sume an idealized hardware environment. There is a critical necessity for
generative models built on state-space architectures capable of mathemati-
cally imputing missing kinematic nodes in real-time when faced with physical
hardware failure.

3.1 System Overview
This research proposes GAE-Mamba-WHAR, a unified deep learning framework
that decouples spatial kinematic extraction from temporal sequence modeling to
resolve latency, spatial blindness, and hardware fragility bottlenecks. Raw multi-
variate time-series data is normalized and passed into a Generative Graph Autoen-
coder (GAE), which acts as an intelligent imputation engine. Utilizing a Graph
Isomorphism Network (GIN), the GAE reconstructs missing sensor nodes from
the anatomical rhythm of surviving nodes. The resulting latent representation
is flattened and processed by a 1D Causal Selective State-Space Model (Mamba)
via parallel scanning. The pipeline concludes with Global Average Pooling (GAP)
and Focal Loss optimization to classify imbalanced activities.

Experimental Setup
To ensure absolute scientific reproducibility and a rigorous empirical evaluation of
the proposed GAE-Mamba-WHAR architecture, this chapter details the bench-
mark datasets, cross-validation protocols, optimized hyperparameter configura-
tions, and the hardware environment utilized during the research.
4.1 Benchmark Datasets
The architecture was evaluated across three publicly available datasets, specifi-
cally selected to stress-test the model across three distinct kinematic challenges:
macroscopic repetitive activities, sporadic and highly imbalanced micro-gestures,
and multi-node topological scaling.
DSADS (Daily and Sports Activities):Serves as the primary benchmark
for macroscopic, periodic kinematics. It captures 19 distinct activities (e.g., walk-
ing, rowing) performed by 8 subjects. Data was collected at 25 Hz utilizing 5 IMU
39

PAGE 40
nodes (Torso, Arms, Legs), yielding a 45-dimensional feature space. Signals were
segmented into 5.0-second sliding windows (125 timesteps) with a 50% overlap,
generating a clean dataset ideal for validating baseline temporal capacity.
OPPORTUNITY Dataset:Widely regarded as an extremely challenging
WHAR benchmark, it evaluates sporadic, functional micro-gestures in a kitchen
environment. Containing recordings from 4 subjects via 5 upper-body IMUs at 30
Hz, its defining challenge is extreme class imbalance—featuring 17 distinct micro-
gestures while over 75% of the timeline is dominated by a ”Null” background class.
Data was processed using 0.8-second windows (24 timesteps) with a 50% overlap
to stress-test Global Average Pooling and Focal Loss mechanisms.
REALDISP (Realistic Sensor Displacement):Utilized to evaluate scal-
ability to deeper, non-Euclidean topologies. It captures 33 fitness activities from
17 subjects using an expanded 9-node sensor array sampled at 50 Hz. Using the
”Ideal Placement” subset, sequences were segmented into 1.0-second windows (50
timesteps) with a 50% overlap to establish the upper-bound accuracy limit for the
Graph Isomorphism Network.
4.2 Validation Protocol: Modified Leave-One-
Subject-Out (LOSO)
A pervasive methodological flaw in contemporary WHAR literature is the reliance
on randomized segment-level train/test splitting. Because sliding windows uti-
lize a 50% overlap, adjacent windows are mathematically correlated. Random
splitting leaks near-identical frames into both sets, causing networks to inadver-
tently memorize biometric kinematic fingerprints rather than generalized semantic
activities.
To ensure strict scientific integrity, prevent data leakage, and prove true zero-
shot cross-subject generalization, all experiments utilized a rigorousModified
40

PAGE 41
Leave-One-Subject-Out (LOSO)protocol. In anN-subject dataset, the data
is partitioned into three distinct subsets per fold:
1.Training Set (N−2 subjects):Used exclusively for weight optimization
and gradient backpropagation.
2.Internal Validation Set (1 subject):Dynamically isolated from the
training pool. Used strictly to calculate internal training accuracy and gov-
ern the Early Stopping callback, guaranteeing stopping criteria are evaluated
on unseen data without polluting the test subject.
3.Holdout Test Set (1 subject):TheN-th subject is kept strictly isolated
for the final zero-shot evaluation (Validation Accuracy and Macro-F1).
This tripartite process iteratesNtimes. The final reported metrics represent
the macroscopic average across all independent folds.

Conclusion and Future Work
6.1 Summary of Achievements
The evolution of Wearable Human Activity Recognition (WHAR) has increasingly de-
manded architectures capable of navigating the complex trilemma of edge deployment:
minimizing inference latency, maximizing cross-subject generalization, and ensuring
strict resilience against physical hardware failure. This research successfully addressed
these critical bottlenecks through the development and empirical validation of the Gen-
erative Graph Autoencoder and Causal Mamba (GAE-Mamba-WHAR) framework.
By mapping physical IMU sensors to non-Euclidean graph topologies via a Graph
Isomorphism Network (GIN), the proposed architecture successfully preserved human
biomechanical kinematics, an element traditionally lost in flat Convolutional Neural
Networks. Furthermore, by replacing legacy Recurrent Neural Network (RNN/GRU)
memory blocks with a 1D Causal Mamba Selective State-Space Model, the framework
eliminated sequential processing bottlenecks. This decoupled spatial-temporal strategy
achieved a 40.6x reduction in inference latency compared to standard bidirectional base-
49

PAGE 50
lines, rendering the model highly optimal for real-time streaming on resource-constrained
microcontrollers.
Finally, the integration of a Generative Graph Autoencoder established a robust, self-
healing imputation mechanism. The network successfully learned the underlying topo-
logical manifold of human motion, allowing the decoder to mathematically hallucinate
missing sensor data via spatial correlation. The resulting architecture achieved highly
competitive zero-shot performance across three diverse benchmark datasets (DSADS,
OPPORTUNITY, and REALDISP), and demonstrated unprecedented robustness by
maintaining high predictive capacity even under simulated catastrophic multi-sensor
failure.
6.2 Technical Limitations
Despite the framework’s significant improvements in speed and hardware robustness,
several technical limitations remain that warrant consideration for real-world deploy-
ment:
1.Static Topological Dependency:The current Graph Isomorphism Network
relies on a statically defined anatomical adjacency matrix (e.g., explicitly linking
the Torso to the Upper Arms). While highly effective for standardized deploy-
ments, the model lacks topological fluidity. If a user introduces a completely novel
sensor placement (e.g., moving a wrist sensor to the chest) that breaks the prede-
fined graph edges, the adjacency matrix must be manually reconfigured and the
network partially retrained.
2.The Causality versus Context Trade-off:To guarantee true real-time stream-
ing capabilities, the Mamba module was strictly constrained to 1D Causal Convo-
lutions. While this prevents illegal ”information leakage” from future timesteps,
it fundamentally limits the model’s ability to utilize post-action context. Conse-
quently, on highly fragmented datasets like OPPORTUNITY, the strictly causal
model trails the absolute peak accuracy of bidirectional state-space models, repre-
senting an unavoidable trade-off between real-time viability and offline contextual
optimization.

6.3 Future Work: Cross-Subject Generalization
While the current framework successfully mitigates hardware-induced domain shifts
(sensor failure and displacement), the most profound challenge remaining in ubiquitous
computing is user-induced domain shift. Human biomechanics are deeply idiosyncratic;
a model trained on the dynamic gait of a young athlete will invariably struggle to gen-
eralize to the kinematic patterns of an elderly patient.
As demonstrated by the generalization gaps observed during the strict Leave-One-
Subject-Out (LOSO) validation, deep learning models inherently attempt to learn user-
specific biometric identifiers (e.g., a specific arm twitch) rather than universal semantic
activities.
To completely eliminate this domain shift, future research will focus on the imple-
mentation of Domain Adversarial Training (DAT). By integrating a Gradient Reversal
Layer (GRL) into the Mamba latent space, the architecture will be forced to undergo a
dual-objective optimization. A secondary classifier will be trained to identify the specific
human subject, while the GRL will invert and backpropagate this loss, actively punish-
ing the primary encoder for retaining biometric data. This adversarial approach will
forcefully strip user identity from the latent representations, compelling the extraction
of universally applicable physical features. Successfully implementing this adversarial
paradigm will pave the way for true, zero-shot generalization to entirely unseen demo-
graphic populations.
